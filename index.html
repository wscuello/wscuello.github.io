About me

I am a theoretical ecologist and applied mathematician whose research focuses on developing mathematical frameworks to understand the long-term behavior of biological systems. My work connects dynamical systems, probability, and ecological theory to study how populations and communities respond to environmental variability, climate change, and life-history tradeoffs.

I am currently an Assistant Professor of Mathematics at Greenfield Community College. I previously held positions at Los Alamos National Laboratory and Rutgers University, and I received my Ph.D. in Applied Mathematics from UC Davis under the guidance of Sebastian Schreiber.

Research

I study complex biological systems using mathematical and computational tools, with a focus on understanding long-term population and community dynamics. My work connects dynamical systems, probability, and game-theoretic ideas to analyze how species adapt and persist under changing environmental conditions.

Much of my work is motivated by ecological systems where environmental variability is unavoidable. For example, Sonoran Desert annual plants provide a natural setting for understanding how organisms respond to extreme fluctuations in precipitation. I have also studied viral life-history strategies, including how latent periods and replication modes may be shaped by ecological parameters in controlled systems such as chemostats.

More broadly, I am interested in mathematical frameworks for persistence, coexistence, adaptation, and long-term statistical behavior in ecological systems.

Teaching

I am a tenure-track professor at Greenfield Community College. My main goal is to make mathematics digestible for students from many socioeconomic backgrounds and career paths. I also serve as an advisor for the STEM Club, where we tackle competitive math problems through AMATYC and explore recreational mathematics and logic puzzles.

I enjoy taking real-world scenarios and finding mathematical ways to approximate their behavior, and I hope to share this enthusiasm with every student who walks through my office or classroom door.
