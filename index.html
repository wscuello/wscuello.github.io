Applied Mathematics · Theoretical Ecology

William S. Chang-Cuello

Applied mathematician studying long-term dynamics in ecological and biological systems.

I develop mathematical and computational frameworks for understanding how populations and communities persist, adapt, and change under environmental variability. My work connects dynamical systems, probability, and ecological theory to study biological systems shaped by uncertainty and climate change.

I am currently an Assistant Professor of Mathematics at Greenfield Community College. I previously held positions at Los Alamos National Laboratory and Rutgers University, and received my Ph.D. in Applied Mathematics from UC Davis under the guidance of Sebastian Schreiber.

Mathematics for biological questions

I am especially interested in how mathematical structure can clarify ecological mechanisms: when populations persist, how strategies become evolutionarily stable, and how long-term community outcomes can be inferred from limited data or simplified descriptions of biological interactions.
